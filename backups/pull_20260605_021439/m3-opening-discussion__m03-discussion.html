MODULE 3 · OPENING DISCUSSION

What is the smallest classifier worth building?

Before you pick your classes, defend a problem.

5 Points · Graded

The Prompt

This week you are going to train a 2-class image classifier. Before you open the tool, you have to defend the problem.

What two classes will you train, who would actually use a classifier between them, and what would 'good enough' look like?

Think about:

• Are your two classes actually distinct? Could a stranger tell them apart from photos?
• Can you realistically capture 30 samples per class this week?
• What is the smallest improvement to someone's life that a correct prediction would make?

Your Task

  1. Initial post (3 to 5 sentences): Name your two classes. State who would use the classifier. Define your 'good enough' bar.
  2. Reply to TWO classmates (2 to 3 sentences each): Pressure-test their class choice. Is it distinct? Is it capturable? Is it useful?
  3. Be specific. No 'cats vs dogs'. No 'spam vs not spam' without context.

What Good Looks Like

Example Responses

STRONG INITIAL POST (Click to expand)

I want to classify 'ripe banana' versus 'overripe banana' for my grandmother, who has low vision and asks family to check her fruit. Both classes are easy for me to photograph in her kitchen across different lights. 'Good enough' for me is 9 out of 10 correct predictions across 20 fresh bananas, because the cost of a wrong answer is at worst a slightly less tasty smoothie.

Why this works: Real user, distinct classes, capturable, honest 'good enough' bar tied to stakes.

STRONG REPLY (Click to expand)

I like the ripe vs overripe idea, but I'd push you to think about what happens when a banana is on the border. If you only train clearly-ripe and clearly-overripe samples, the model will be confidently wrong on borderline fruit, which is exactly when your grandmother needs help most. Maybe capture some 'borderline' samples and decide which class they belong to up front.

Why this works: Engages with the specific scenario, identifies a real edge case, proposes a concrete fix.

WEAK POST (Click to expand)

I will train cat vs dog. It will be useful.

Why this loses points: No user, no 'good enough' bar, no honest reflection on whether the problem is interesting.
