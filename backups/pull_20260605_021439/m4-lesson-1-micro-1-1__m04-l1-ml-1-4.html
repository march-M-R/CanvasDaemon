What Is Algorithmic Fairness?

Fairness is a design choice. There are at least three definitions.

Big Question

“When someone says an algorithm is fair, what do they actually mean?”

Concept Companion

Three definitions, three trade-offs.

Each of these three definitions has its own measure, its own audit, and its own cost. You cannot satisfy all three at once. You can only choose which trade-offs you are making.

1

Equal outcomes

Group

Similar error rates across protected groups. Audit by disaggregating accuracy by group.

2

Similar people, similar predictions

Individual

Two applicants with similar profiles should get similar decisions.

3

Fair process

Procedural

People can see, contest, and appeal the model's decision.

Key Insight

Fairness has multiple definitions.

Group fairness, individual fairness, and procedural fairness all sound like the same thing. They are not. They often conflict. Whoever picks one is implicitly trading away the others.

Compare

When the definitions disagree.

Real cases where pursuing one fairness definition meant abandoning another. There is no neutral choice.

Optimizing for...

Equal group accuracy

Often costs...

Some individuals get worse predictions

Real example

Lending models tuned to match group approval rates

Optimizing for...

Equal individual treatment

Often costs...

Some groups bear higher error rates

Real example

Hiring models that score 'similar resumes' the same way

Optimizing for...

Procedural transparency

Often costs...

Speed and scale

Real example

Explainable models often less accurate than black-box ones
