The Core Question

Oversight is a design choice, not a default.

Every AI system places humans somewhere in the decision loop. The choice of where, and how much authority that human has, shapes every outcome. 'Just review everything' is rarely realistic. 'Trust the model' is rarely safe.

Animation representing human review and oversight

Oversight Defined

A person, named in advance, with the authority to review the model's decision and override it where appropriate.

Three Levels of Human Oversight Click to expand or collapse this section.

Pick your oversight

Different levels for different stakes.

Not all human oversight is the same. The choice of level depends on the stakes of being wrong, the volume of decisions, and how confident the model is on individual predictions.

Strongest oversight

Every decision

A human reviews and approves every output. Slow. Used in safety, health, sentencing.

Medium oversight

Sampled review

A human spot-checks a percentage of decisions. Catches drift over time.

Lightest oversight

Triggered review

A human only sees decisions the model flags as uncertain or risky.

Key Point: The danger is drifting into 'no oversight' by accident. The choice of level should be made explicitly, in writing, before launch, not after a complaint.

Where Review Is Required vs Optional The four domains where automation alone is not safe.

Some AI systems can ship with light oversight. Some cannot. The question is not whether the model is accurate. It is whether the cost of a wrong decision can be carried by the affected person.

Review Required

Domains

Health, safety, legal, financial, hiring

Mandatory human review.

Decisions that affect health, freedom, financial well-being, or employment. The cost of being wrong falls on the person, not the model. A human must review and have the power to override.

Review Optional

Domains

Recommendations, sorting, autocomplete

Light or sampled review.

Low-stakes, recoverable decisions. A wrong song recommendation is mildly annoying. A wrong photo tag is fixable. Light oversight is acceptable, and sometimes desirable for usability.
The AI Risk Review Open this to see the three-question framework.

One Page, Three Questions

Specific answers. No theater.

Before any AI system touches a real decision, the team behind it should answer three questions in writing. Vague answers do not count. Specific answers are the deliverable.

Pre-Launch Review

Done before launch, not after. The discipline is naming the harm before it happens, not explaining it after.

Question 1

What can go wrong? Specific failure modes, not 'the model could be biased.'

Question 2

Who is hurt? Specific groups: applicants, patients, students, communities. Not 'users.'

Question 3

What are we doing about it? Concrete mitigations: human review thresholds, opt-outs, audit cadences.

Sign and date

The team that signs the review is the team responsible if a listed harm occurs.

Harms: Named, specific, in writing.

Groups: Named, specific, not abstract.

Mitigations: Concrete actions, not platitudes.

Accountability: Someone signs the page.

Four Triggers That Force a Human Review Open this to see the situations where automation alone is irresponsible.

The Non-Negotiables

Four cases. No exceptions.

Some situations require a human to review every decision, even when the model's overall accuracy is high. The accuracy number does not change the math on these cases.

Trigger 1

Health, safety, or life

Medical diagnosis, drug dosing, autonomous driving. The cost of being wrong is irreversible.

Trigger 2

Legal status or freedom

Sentencing, parole, immigration decisions. Wrong here means lost rights.

Trigger 3

Significant financial decision

Loans, hiring, housing. Wrong here shapes someone's economic future.

Trigger 4

Decisions about minors

Anything affecting a person who cannot consent to the system reviewing them.

Result: If a system touches any of these four, human review is mandatory regardless of model accuracy. No exception is safe.

Common Oversight Misconceptions Open this section to review the traps.

'Higher accuracy means we can drop human review'

Accuracy is one dimension. The cost of remaining errors, who bears it, and whether it is reversible are the questions that decide oversight level.

'Triggered review is enough'

It is sometimes. But the trigger has to actually catch the risky cases. A model that does not know what it does not know cannot flag itself reliably.

'The team doing the review is the same team that built it'

Conflict of interest. Effective review usually involves an independent reviewer who did not build the model.

Key Takeaways

What you should remember from Lesson 4

'Human in the loop' means three different things. Every decision reviewed, sampled review, or triggered review. Pick on purpose.

High-stakes domains require non-negotiable review. Health, safety, legal, and financial decisions cannot be left to a model alone.

An AI risk review is one page, three questions. Harms, affected groups, mitigations. Specific or it is theater.
