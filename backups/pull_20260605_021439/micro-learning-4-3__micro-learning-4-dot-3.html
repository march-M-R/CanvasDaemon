Warning Signs

Red flags in AI answers

No source

Specific factual claims appear without evidence.

Too confident

The answer sounds certain when the topic is uncertain.

Fake details

Names, dates, quotes, or citations may be invented.

Outdated info

The answer may miss recent changes or updates.

Key idea: Hallucinations are not always obvious. The most dangerous ones often sound professional and believable.

Verification Toolkit

How to respond to a suspicious answer

Step Action Why it helps
1 Identify the factual claim. You cannot verify a vague impression; you verify specific claims.
2 Ask for or find a reliable source. Reliable sources reduce the risk of made-up information.
3 Check the date and context. Some answers become wrong when information changes.
4 Revise or reject the answer if needed. Human judgment remains part of responsible AI use.
Open: What counts as a hallucination?

An AI hallucination is an output that sounds plausible but is not grounded in correct information. It may include a wrong fact, fake citation, invented quote, incorrect explanation, or misleading connection.

The important point is not whether the answer sounds polished. The important point is whether it can be checked and supported.

Meme-Level Summary

“Source: trust me bro” is not a citation.

If an answer includes facts that matter, check where those facts came from before using them.
