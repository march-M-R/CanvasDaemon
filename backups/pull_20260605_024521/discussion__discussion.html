Opening Discussion: Who's Really in Control?

LECTURE 1 · OPENING DISCUSSION

Who's Really in Control?

Before we learn the framework, test your thinking

5 Points · Graded

Before you learn the Three Forms framework, we need to establish your baseline thinking. This discussion isn't about being "right"—it's about capturing where you are now. You'll return to this at the end of the module and see how your understanding evolved.

The Question

This morning, your Instagram feed showed you 20 posts. You didn't choose which posts or what order. Instagram's algorithm decided for you based on what it predicted you'd engage with.

Who was in control of what you saw: you, or the algorithm?

Think about:

• Did you decide what to see, or did Instagram decide for you?
• Could you have seen those posts without the algorithm?
• If you can't choose the order, are you still in control?
• Is this human intelligence, machine intelligence, or something in between?

What to Submit

Discussion Instructions

📝 Your Task

  1. Answer the question in 3-5 sentences. Who was in control—you or the algorithm? Explain your reasoning. There's no single right answer, but you need to defend your position.
  2. Reply to TWO classmates. Each reply should be at least 2-3 sentences. Did they think about control differently than you? What did their answer make you reconsider?
  3. Be specific. Don't just say "I agree" or "Good point." Explain WHY you agree, or add something new to their thinking.

Why This Matters: The question seems simple, but there's no obvious answer. That's the point. You're establishing your intuition before we give you the framework to analyze it properly.

What Good Looks Like

Example Responses

Strong Initial Post (Full Points)

AK
Alex K.
Posted 2 hours ago

I think I'm in control, but barely. Instagram decides which 20 posts I see out of potentially hundreds, and it orders them based on what kept me scrolling yesterday. I never asked for that order—it just happened. So while I can scroll past posts I don't like, I'm only choosing from options the algorithm already filtered for me. It's like being handed a menu with only 5 items when the kitchen can make 100 dishes. I'm "choosing," but someone else decided what I get to choose from.

So my answer: the algorithm controls what I see, but I control whether I engage with it. We're both involved, but Instagram has way more power in this situation than I do.

Why this works: Specific reasoning, acknowledges complexity, makes a clear claim, uses a concrete metaphor (menu analogy), defends the position with evidence.

Strong Reply (Full Points)

MR
Maya R.
Posted 1 hour ago · Reply to Alex K.

I love your menu analogy, but I disagree that you're in control of whether you engage. Instagram also decides how long each post stays on your screen and what shows up next based on how you reacted to the last one. So even your "choice" to engage is being shaped by decisions Instagram already made. You're not just choosing from a filtered menu—the waiter is also controlling how fast the dishes arrive and what comes next based on what you ate first.

Why this works: Engages directly with classmate's idea, respectfully disagrees, extends the metaphor, adds new insight.

Weak Post (Loses Points)

JL
Jordan L.
Posted 3 hours ago

I think the algorithm is in control because it decides what I see. AI is really powerful and Instagram uses it to keep us scrolling.

Why this loses points: Too short (2 sentences), vague reasoning ("AI is powerful"), no specific evidence, doesn't engage with the complexity of the question.

💡 Tips for Success

  • Don't overthink it. We haven't taught you the framework yet. Use your intuition.
  • Be specific. Reference actual features of Instagram (feed order, recommendations, etc.)
  • Defend your position. Whatever you claim, explain WHY you think that.
  • Engage genuinely in replies. "I agree" is not a reply. Add something new or challenge their thinking.
  • Post early. You need to reply to TWO classmates, which is easier if there are already posts to reply to.
