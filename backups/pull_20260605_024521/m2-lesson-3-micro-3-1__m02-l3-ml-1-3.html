Why Models Need Data They Haven't Seen

Memorizing is not learning. The gap shows up in the test score.

Big Question

“Why is 99 percent on the training set usually a bad sign?”

Concept Companion

Generalize, not memorize.

Generalization means the model captures the rule that produced the data, not just the specific examples it saw. Memorization is the failure mode where the model rote-learns the answers.

1

Rote-learn the training set

Memorize

Training accuracy: very high. Test accuracy: low. The model 'aced the practice exam' and failed the real one.

2

Did not learn enough

Underfit

Training accuracy: medium. Test accuracy: medium. The model needs more capacity, more data, or better features.

3

Captured the real pattern

Generalize

Training accuracy: high. Test accuracy: almost as high. This is the goal. Boring is good.

Key Insight

High training score, low test score, the model memorized.

A model is supposed to learn the underlying pattern, not the specific examples. The way you tell the difference: hold some examples back, never let the model see them during training, and check how the model does on those held-out examples.

Compare

Reading two numbers tells the whole story.

Every honest ML team reports both training and test accuracy. The gap between them is the most important number in the report.
