LECTURE 1 · OPENING DISCUSSION

Who's Really in Control?

Before we learn the framework, test your thinking

5 Points · Graded

When the algorithm curates your choices... who's really deciding?

The Question

Last night, you opened Netflix. Your homepage showed you 20 recommended shows. You didn't choose which ones or what order. Netflix's algorithm decided for you.

Who decided what you watched: you, or Netflix?

Think about:

• Did you decide, or did Netflix decide for you?
• If Netflix controls what you see, are you still making the choice?
• Is this human intelligence, machine intelligence, or something in between?

Your Task

  1. Answer in 3 to 5 sentences. Who was in control? Defend your position.
  2. Reply to TWO classmates. Engage with their ideas, 2 to 3 sentences each.
  3. Be specific. Reference actual Netflix features, not vague statements.

What Good Looks Like

Example Responses

STRONG INITIAL POST (Click to expand)

Sarah C. · 2 hours ago

Netflix is in control of what I can choose from. When I open the app, it's already decided which 20 titles to show me based on my watch history and what I scrolled past. I never asked for that lineup. So while I can pick from those 20 options, Netflix eliminated hundreds of others I'll never know existed. Netflix controls the menu, I control whether I order from it.

Why this works: Specific reasoning, clear position, uses evidence such as watch history and scrolling.

STRONG REPLY (Click to expand)

Marcus K. · Reply to Sarah C.

Sarah, I'd push back on one thing: Netflix also controls HOW the menu is presented. If it puts a show in the #1 spot with an attention-grabbing thumbnail, you're way more likely to click it than if it was in row 5. So Netflix isn't just controlling what's on the menu, it's nudging you toward specific choices.

Why this works: Engages directly with her point, adds new insight, and respectfully challenges.

WEAK POST (Click to expand)

Jamie L. · 3 hours ago

Netflix is in control because it uses AI. The algorithm is really powerful.

Why this loses points: Too short, vague reasoning, no evidence, and does not engage with complexity.
