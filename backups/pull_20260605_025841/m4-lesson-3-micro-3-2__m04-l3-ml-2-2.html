Confident and Correct Are Not the Same

Tone is not evidence. Style is not citation.

Big Question

Why is a confident answer especially dangerous?
Watch → Read → Apply

Before You Watch

Confidence shuts down questioning.

When a model writes with full certainty, readers stop double-checking. That is exactly when verification matters most. The most dangerous LLM output is the confident one that happens to be wrong.

Concept Companion

Four combinations of confidence and truth.

Each cell calls for a different response. The most common mistake is treating all four the same.

Useful

Confident + correct

Verify anyway for important decisions. Don't get lazy.

Dangerous

Confident + wrong

Most expensive failure mode. Looks like an authoritative answer and is not.

Honest

Hedged + correct

The model signaled its uncertainty. Trust the hedge, verify when stakes warrant.

Compare

How confidence and truth combine.

Two axes. Four cells. Each requires a different response. The most expensive mistake is to act on the cells uniformly.

ToneTruthWhat to do
ConfidentRightAcceptable. Verify for high stakes.
ConfidentWrongWorst case. Verify every confident claim.
HedgedRightUseful. The model warned you fairly.
HedgedWrongPredictable. Easier to forgive.
