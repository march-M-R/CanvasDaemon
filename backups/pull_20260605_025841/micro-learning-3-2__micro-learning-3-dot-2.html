Recommendation AI

Recommendation systems decide what to rank, suggest, and show next, often shaping what people watch, buy, read, and listen to.

Big Question

How do platforms decide what to recommend?
Watch → Rank → Reflect

Before You Watch

Think about your “For You” page.

Your feed is not random. It is usually ranked using signals such as likes, watch time, clicks, skips, saves, follows, and similarity.

What does the platform seem to know about your preferences?

Open a hint

The system may not “know” you personally. It may be comparing your behavior to patterns from many users and many items.

Recommendation signals

Explicit

Likes, ratings, follows, saves, playlist adds.

Implicit

Watch time, skips, replays, clicks, scrolling behavior.

Similarity

Similar users, similar songs, similar videos, similar items.

Context

Time, location, device, trend, topic, or recent activity.

Key idea: A recommendation is usually not one single guess. It is a ranked prediction based on many signals.

Compare

Recommendation is not the same as search

System What you do What AI may do
Search You type a query. Ranks results based on relevance, popularity, quality, and context.
Recommendation You may not ask directly. Predicts what you may want next based on patterns and behavior.
Feed You scroll. Continuously updates what appears based on engagement signals.
Open: Why recommendations can feel addictive

Recommendation systems can continuously learn from behavior. When they optimize for engagement, they may keep showing content that holds attention.

That can be useful when the content is helpful or entertaining. But it can also become distracting, repetitive, or narrow if the system mostly rewards what keeps people watching longer.

Meme-Level Summary

“I watched one cooking video and now my entire feed thinks I’m opening a restaurant.”

That is recommendation AI reacting to behavior signals and trying to predict what should appear next.
