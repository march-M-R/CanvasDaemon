Concept Companion

Three ways intelligence can show up.

The video introduces the idea. This section helps you apply it using examples you already know.

Form 1

Human Intelligence

The ability of a person to learn, understand, reason, adapt, and solve problems using experience and judgement.

Example: You change your study plan after realizing flashcards are not helping enough.

Form 2

Artificial Intelligence

The ability of a machine system to perform tasks that normally require intelligence, often by using data and patterns.

Example: A music or video app recommends something new based on patterns in what you watch or skip.

Form 3

Collective Intelligence

The intelligence that emerges when groups combine knowledge, perspectives, and coordination to solve problems.

Example: A class shared study guide improves as students add notes, catch mistakes, and explain ideas to each other.

Compare

Same word, different source.

Type Where it comes from Strength Limitation
Human intelligence Individual experience, reasoning, memory, emotion, and judgement Flexible, contextual, creative Can be biased, tired, distracted, or limited by personal experience
Artificial intelligence Data, algorithms, models, and computing power Fast, scalable, good at pattern-heavy tasks Does not understand the world exactly like humans do
Collective intelligence Groups, communities, networks, collaboration, and shared knowledge Combines many perspectives and areas of expertise Can suffer from groupthink, misinformation, or coordination problems
