Limitations and Bias in AI

AI systems can be useful, but they are shaped by their data, design choices, and the situations where people use them.

Big Question

Why can an AI system be technically impressive but still unfair, incomplete, or wrong?
Data · Design · Impact

Before You Watch

AI is only as good as the system behind it.

A model can be powerful and still make mistakes if the training data is limited, biased, outdated, or not representative.

What could go wrong if an AI system learns from incomplete examples?

Open a hint

If the examples are missing certain groups, situations, or perspectives, the AI may perform worse for those cases.

Common Limits

Four limits to watch for

Incomplete data

The system may not have seen enough examples of certain cases.

Biased data

The data may reflect unfair patterns from the real world.

Wrong context

A model may be used in a situation it was not designed for.

Overtrust

People may treat AI output as final without checking it.

Key idea: Bias does not always mean someone intentionally made the system unfair. It can come from data, design, deployment, or how people interpret the output.

Bias Pathway

How bias can enter an AI system

Stage What can happen Question to ask
Data collection Some groups or cases may be missing or underrepresented. Who is included and who is missing?
Training The model may learn unfair patterns from historical data. What patterns is the model learning?
Testing The system may perform well overall but poorly for specific groups. Does performance differ across cases?
Deployment People may use the system in ways the designers did not expect. What happens when real people rely on it?
Open: Why “average accuracy” can hide problems

A system can look accurate overall but still perform poorly for a smaller group or less common situation.

That is why responsible AI testing should look beyond one overall score. It should ask where the system works, where it fails, and who is affected.

Meme-Level Summary

“The AI passed the test… but whose test was it?”

Responsible AI means asking whether a system works fairly and reliably across the people and situations it affects.
