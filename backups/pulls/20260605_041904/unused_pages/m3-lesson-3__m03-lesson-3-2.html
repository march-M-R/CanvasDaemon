Real-World Story

The hospital X-ray watermark

In 2020, researchers reviewed a wave of published COVID X-ray classifiers, almost all claiming 95%+ accuracy. When they audited the models, the majority were not looking at the lungs at all. They were reading hospital identifiers in the corner of each scan. Hospital A had treated severe cases; Hospital B was a screening clinic. The models 'diagnosed' the watermark, not the patient. None were safe to deploy.

Core Idea

If you only test on training data, you have not tested.

A model that scores 100 percent on its training data could still fail on anything new. Real testing uses fresh samples the model has never seen.

Honest testing is the only kind that matters.

Quick Reference

Four Outcomes of a Binary Classifier

Every prediction lands in one of these boxes.

OutcomeMeaningTypical cost
True positiveModel said 'yes' and was rightLow; this is the good case
False positiveModel said 'yes' and was wrongWrong alarm; cost depends on context
False negativeModel said 'no' and was wrongMissed detection; often costlier in safety
True negativeModel said 'no' and was rightLow; correct rejection

Key Takeaways

What you should remember

Test on at least 10 fresh samples per class, none of them from training.

Log every prediction with its confidence score, even the ones the model gets right.

A confusion matrix tells you which class the model confuses with which.
