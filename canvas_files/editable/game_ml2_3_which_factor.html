Your choice More Data

Strongest case for

Without data, algorithms have nothing to learn from. Every AI breakthrough from image recognition to language models required datasets that simply did not exist before the internet, smartphones, and social platforms generated them at scale. Data is the raw material everything else depends on.

Strongest case against

Large datasets existed before modern AI succeeded. ImageNet was created in 2009 but the breakthrough came in 2012 with AlexNet's architecture. The data was available; what changed was the algorithm and the hardware to train it. Data alone is not sufficient without the tools to use it.

The nuance: Data, compute, and algorithms are interdependent. What makes the data argument strong is that it is the hardest factor to manufacture artificially. You can buy more GPUs and publish better algorithms, but you cannot easily fabricate decades of genuine human behaviour at internet scale.
Your choice More Compute

Strongest case for

Many of the algorithms behind modern AI, including neural networks, have existed since the 1980s. What changed is that GPUs made it feasible to train them at useful scale. Compute is what made the theoretical practical. Without it, data and algorithms stay academic.

Strongest case against

More compute applied to the wrong algorithm produces expensive failures. The architecture innovations of the 2010s, particularly convolutional and transformer models, were necessary to use that compute effectively. Raw processing power without the right structure does not produce intelligence.

The nuance: Compute is often the limiting factor in practice. When researchers have a good algorithm and good data but cannot afford to train the model, progress stops. The democratisation of GPU access through cloud platforms is one reason AI development accelerated rapidly after 2015.
Your choice Better Algorithms

Strongest case for

The 2012 ImageNet moment was not about new data or hardware alone. AlexNet's convolutional architecture outperformed everything before it by a large margin using existing resources. Later, the transformer architecture unlocked language models. The algorithm is what decides whether resources get used effectively.

Strongest case against

Transformers trained on small datasets with limited compute produce weak models. The same architecture that powers GPT-4 produces much less impressive results without billions of training examples and vast compute clusters. A better algorithm is only as good as the data and compute behind it.

The nuance: Algorithm improvements have a multiplying effect. A 10x better algorithm can achieve the same results with 10x less data or compute. This is why architectural breakthroughs feel so significant: they change what is possible given the same resources.
Your choice Connected Platforms

Strongest case for

AI that cannot reach users has no impact. Connected platforms created both the data pipelines to train AI and the distribution channels to deploy it to billions of people instantly. Without platforms, AI stays in research labs. The feedback loop between deployment and improvement is what drives rapid progress.

Strongest case against

Platforms are the delivery mechanism, not the engine. A social media app cannot make a weak AI model perform better. The underlying capability has to exist first. Distribution amplifies AI but does not create it. The technical foundations had to come first.

The nuance: Connected platforms created something the other factors did not: a feedback loop. Real-world usage at scale generates new data, which improves models, which improves products, which attracts more users. This compounding dynamic is a genuine structural advantage that pure research cannot replicate.
