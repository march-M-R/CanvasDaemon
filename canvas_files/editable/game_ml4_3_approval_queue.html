Email

An email system routes an incoming message to the spam folder without human review.

The email contains phrases common in promotional spam. The filter has a 98.5% accuracy rate. The user can check their spam folder and mark messages as not spam at any time.

Stakes: If wrong, the user misses an email but can find it in spam and restore it. Fully reversible.

Ruling

Approved. AI oversight is appropriate here.

Spam filtering is a well-established, low-stakes, fully reversible decision. The error rate is low and when errors occur, users can correct them immediately. Requiring human review of every spam classification would defeat the purpose of the system entirely. This is exactly the kind of task AI can handle autonomously: high volume, low stakes, easy to reverse.

Principle: High accuracy, low stakes, and fully reversible errors with user-accessible correction make this appropriate for autonomous AI action.

Ruling

Approved is the better call here.

Human review of every spam decision would be operationally impossible and would provide minimal benefit. The decision is reversible, the accuracy rate is very high, and users have a clear path to correct errors. Reserve escalation for decisions that are high-stakes, irreversible, or affect individual rights. Spam filtering does not meet that threshold.

Principle: Escalation should be proportionate to stakes. Spam filtering errors are minor and self-correctable. This does not meet the threshold for mandatory human review.

Finance

An AI system declines a loan application and sends the applicant an automated rejection with no explanation.

The applicant has a borderline credit profile. The model's decision was based on 47 input variables. The applicant cannot appeal because they have not been told what factors led to the rejection.

Stakes: The applicant may lose access to needed credit and cannot challenge the decision without knowing why it was made.

Ruling

Escalate is the better call here.

A loan rejection affects a person's financial access in a way that is difficult to reverse. Without an explanation, the applicant cannot appeal. The model used 47 variables and its reasoning is opaque. This combination of high stakes, limited transparency, and no correction path requires human oversight. In many jurisdictions, automated credit decisions without explanation are also legally problematic.

Principle: Decisions that affect individual economic rights, are difficult to reverse, and lack a transparent appeal process require human involvement.

Ruling

Escalated. Human review required.

Correct. A loan rejection has real financial consequences and the applicant has no basis to challenge it without knowing why it was made. The model's reasoning is opaque across 47 variables. Financial decisions affecting individual access to credit require human oversight and an accessible explanation when the decision goes against the applicant.

Principle: Consequential, hard-to-reverse decisions affecting individual rights require human review, especially when the AI's reasoning cannot be explained to the person affected.

Streaming

A streaming platform's AI selects which 30 films appear on a user's home screen, based on their viewing history.

The platform has 5,000 films. The AI ranks all 5,000 and surfaces the 30 most likely to be watched. The user can search for any film at any time.

Stakes: If the ranking is poor, the user sees less relevant options. They can search for anything they want. No individual rights are affected.

Ruling

Approved. AI oversight is appropriate here.

Content recommendation is a low-stakes, preference-based, reversible decision. If the AI makes a poor recommendation, the user watches something less enjoyable or searches for something else. No individual rights are affected. The user retains full access to all content. Human review of every content recommendation for every user would be impossible and entirely out of proportion to the stakes.

Principle: Preference-based, reversible decisions with no impact on rights or safety can appropriately be made by AI without human review.

Ruling

Approved is the better call here.

Content recommendations carry minimal risk. A poor recommendation results in a slightly less enjoyable experience, not harm. The user can always search for what they want. Human oversight should be reserved for decisions with meaningful potential for harm. Personalised content ranking does not meet that threshold.

Principle: Match the level of oversight to the stakes. Low-stakes, easily-correctable decisions do not require human review.

Healthcare

An AI diagnostic tool analyses a patient's scan and concludes there is no sign of disease. The result is filed without a radiologist reviewing the scan.

The tool has 94% accuracy on its test set. The model was trained on a different hospital system's patient population. The clinic's patients differ in age and demographic profile from the training data.

Stakes: A missed diagnosis may mean the patient does not receive treatment they need. This could be irreversible or seriously harmful.

Ruling

Escalate is the better call here.

A missed diagnosis can have serious, irreversible health consequences. The model was trained on a different patient population, raising real questions about whether its 94% accuracy applies in this specific clinic's context. Medical decisions with individual health consequences require human review, particularly when the AI's training does not match the deployment population. A 6% error rate in high-stakes medical decisions is not negligible.

Principle: High-stakes medical decisions, especially from models not validated on the current population, require human oversight regardless of reported accuracy figures.

Ruling

Escalated. Human review required.

Correct. The model was trained on a different patient population and has not been validated on this clinic's demographics. A missed diagnosis is potentially irreversible. In high-stakes medical settings, AI should be used as a tool to assist human clinicians, not to replace their review, particularly when the training context does not match the deployment context.

Principle: Irreversible decisions affecting individual health require human oversight. A mismatch between training population and deployment population reinforces this further.

Infrastructure

An AI traffic management system adjusts signal timing across a city's intersections in real time based on live sensor data.

The system updates signal timings every 30 seconds. A human traffic controller monitors the dashboard and can override any intersection at any time. The system has been operating reliably for 18 months.

Stakes: If a signal timing is suboptimal, traffic flow is reduced. A human controller can intervene immediately. No individual rights are at stake in routine operation.

Ruling

Approved. AI oversight is appropriate here.

Traffic signal optimisation is a well-bounded, continuously monitored decision with human override available at all times. The system has a proven track record. Suboptimal signal timing results in slower traffic, not harm to individuals. A human controller can intervene at any intersection immediately. This is a well-designed human-in-the-loop system where AI handles routine decisions and humans retain the ability to step in when needed.

Principle: AI autonomy is appropriate when a human with override authority is actively monitoring, individual error stakes are low, and the system has demonstrated reliable performance.

Ruling

Approved is the better call here.

A human controller is actively monitoring and can override any decision. The consequences of a suboptimal signal timing are minor. The system has performed reliably for 18 months. Requiring human approval for every signal change every 30 seconds would make the system unworkable and would likely produce worse outcomes than the AI alone. Continuous monitoring with override capability is itself a form of meaningful human oversight.

Principle: Not every AI decision requires pre-approval. Continuous human monitoring with override capability is a legitimate form of oversight for lower-stakes, high-frequency decisions.

Criminal Justice

An AI risk assessment tool rates a prisoner as high risk of reoffending. A parole board uses this score as the primary basis to deny parole without additional review of the individual's case.

The tool was designed to assist human decision-makers, not replace them. The prisoner has served the minimum required sentence. The score is based on group-level statistical patterns, not the individual's specific circumstances or behaviour during their sentence.

Stakes: The prisoner remains incarcerated longer. The AI score is based on statistical averages, not individual circumstances. The decision is irreversible without a formal appeal.

Ruling

Escalate is the better call here.

An AI risk score based on statistical group patterns is being used as the primary basis to deny an individual their liberty. The tool was designed to assist humans, not replace them. Using it as the primary decision factor, without reviewing the person's individual circumstances, removes the human accountability this type of decision requires. Liberty is a fundamental right. Decisions affecting it must involve meaningful human judgment.

Principle: Decisions affecting fundamental rights such as liberty must involve human judgment that considers individual circumstances, not just statistical group patterns.

Ruling

Escalated. Human review required.

Correct. A parole decision affects a person's liberty and is one of the highest-stakes decisions any institution makes. The AI score is based on statistical group patterns and cannot account for the individual's specific behaviour, circumstances, or personal development. The tool was explicitly designed to assist human decision-makers, not to replace them. Using it as the sole basis for denial is a misuse that removes the human accountability this decision demands.

Principle: AI tools designed to assist humans should never become the sole basis for decisions that affect fundamental rights. Human accountability is not optional in these contexts.

Queue complete.

Human oversight is not about reviewing everything. It is about matching the level of review to the stakes. Routine, reversible, low-risk decisions can be automated. Consequential, irreversible decisions affecting individual rights require human accountability.
