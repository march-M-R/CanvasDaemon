Module 2 Lesson 2 · Big Activity

Features and Labels Workshop

Below is a small dataset of 6 example student-record-style rows. Your job: name the prediction target, choose three features that carry signal, label each row, and submit a one-page Features and Labels Worksheet you can save to your portfolio. The worksheet is the deliverable. The grading rubric is below.

Step 1

Define the prediction target

What is the model trying to predict? Write a one-sentence target. Example: "Predict whether a student will complete a course on time."

Step 2

Pick three features

From the columns below, pick three you think carry signal. Then write one sentence explaining why each one helps the model.

homework_completion_rate avg_quiz_score attendance_rate forum_posts_per_week days_since_last_login eye_color zip_code student_id
Green tags are likely-useful columns. Red tags are noise, proxies, or unethical to use.
Step 3

Label each row

Six rows of student-record-style data. Pick the label for each based on your target. The data is fictional. You decide whether each student should be predicted as "on track" or "at risk".

rowhomework_completionavg_quiz_scoreattendance_rateforum_postsdays_since_loginYOUR LABEL
There is no single correct label. Make a defensible decision per row based on your target. Differences between students are intentional.
Step 4

Name one bias risk in your labels

Even with a small dataset, your labeling choices reflect assumptions. Name one risk: a kind of student your labels might misclassify, a missing column that would change your decisions, or a proxy that snuck in.
