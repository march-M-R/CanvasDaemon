Big Summary Question

When is an agent justified, and what must remain limited, visible, and reversible?

Module Story

Connect the four lessons.

Coordinating a student-club event involves goals, schedules, messages, decisions, and stopping before taking an action you are not authorized to take. An AI agent similarly chooses steps, uses tools, observes results, updates its state, and continues toward a stopping condition. More tool access increases both capability and the consequences of mistakes.

Four Big Ideas

The module in one view

1. What Makes an AI an Agent?

Separate chatbots, workflows, and agents by looking for goals, decisions, tools, and action over time.

2. Tools, Goals, and Why Agents Need Limits

Connect tools to goals, then explain why permissions, scope, and human checkpoints keep agent actions controlled.

3. How an Agent Plans: Observe, Think, Act

Trace the agent loop from observation to planning to action, and spot where a missed step can cause failure.

4. Guardrails, Checkpoints, and Keeping Agents on Track

Use hard limits, soft limits, and review points to decide when an agent should continue, pause, or escalate.

Key Takeaways

What should stay with you

A chatbot responds, a workflow follows a fixed path, and an agent can choose among next actions.

Least privilege gives the system only the data and actions required for its job.

Traces, stopping rules, and layered guardrails make behavior inspectable and recoverable.

High-impact or irreversible actions require meaningful human approval.
