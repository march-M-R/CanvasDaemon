MODULE 14 · OPENING DISCUSSION

Should This Be an Agent?

Before the lesson gives you a framework, make the first call yourself.

Required · Initial post + two replies

The Question

A student group wants an AI system to help organize an event. It could answer questions, follow a fixed checklist, or act as an agent that uses tools and completes steps on the group's behalf.

What is the smallest suitable system for this task, and which actions must still require human approval?

Think about:

• Would a chatbot, fixed workflow, agent, or hybrid be enough?
• Which tool or permission would the system need?
• What evidence could lead you to choose do not build?

Your Task

  1. Write an initial post of 3 to 5 sentences. Take a clear position and support it with one reason or example.
  2. Reply to two classmates in 2 to 3 sentences each. Ask a question, test an assumption, or add evidence. Do more than say you agree.
  3. Be specific. Refer to scope, tools, permissions, checkpoints, fallbacks, or human approval. Use fictional information only.

What Good Looks Like

Example Responses

STRONG INITIAL POST (select to expand)

I would start with a fixed workflow that drafts a schedule and checklist, not an agent that can send messages. The system may read fictional event details, but it should not receive access to student records or personal accounts. A person must approve every external message. If the workflow cannot reliably distinguish draft information from confirmed details, I would not automate that step.

Why this works: Clear position, specific course evidence, and an honest limitation.

STRONG REPLY (select to expand)

Your fixed workflow is safer than a fully independent agent, but the draft could still contain an incorrect date. I would add a checkpoint that compares every event detail with the approved plan. What should happen if the sources disagree?

Why this works: Responds directly, adds a new consideration, and ends with a useful question.

WEAK POST (select to expand)

I would use an agent because it can do everything automatically.

Why this does not meet the requirements: Too short, vague, and unsupported by specific evidence.
