What Counts as Data (and What Doesn't)

If a machine can load it and compare it, it is data. If it lives in your head, it is not.

Big Question

What is the difference between a feeling, a guess, and data?
Watch → Read → Apply

Before You Watch

Data has to be captured.

A feeling about a song, a hunch about a stock, a sense that a friend is upset, none of these become data until they are written down in a form that another person or a machine can read.

Concept Companion

Recorded. Comparable. Loadable.

Three properties decide whether something counts as data. The properties matter more than the topic. Numbers, text, images, audio, video all qualify, as long as they are captured in a consistent form.

Captured outside your head

Recorded

Written, photographed, measured. A vibe is not data. A vibe noted down with a date and a song is.

Same form across examples

Comparable

Every row has the same columns. Every photo loads the same way. Every audio clip is the same kind of waveform.

Machine can read it

Loadable

Numbers, text, pixels, samples. Anything a program can pull into memory and compare with another example.

Compare

Three things people often confuse for data.

All three feel like data because they involve numbers or observations. Two of them are not, until they go through one more step.

SourceData?What it actually is
A gut feeling about a videoNot yetAn impression. Data the moment you write down the video ID and your guess.
A folder of CSV files exported from an appYesAlready in rows and columns. Ready to load.
A conversation with a friendNot yetWords spoken. Data only after audio, transcript, or notes are made.
