MODULE 2 · OPENING DISCUSSION

Should This AI Get a Say?

Before we study data, test what makes examples fair.

5 Points · Graded

When AI ranks people, the data decides what counts as potential.

The Question

Your school is choosing students for a paid summer internship. There are more applicants than spots, so someone suggests using AI to make the first shortlist. Student A trains the AI using 30 past interns who were rated as successful by the company. Student B trains the AI using 30 students who applied, including students who were accepted, rejected, and never got an interview.

Which dataset would you trust more, and what could still go wrong?

Think about:

• What does each dataset represent, and who is missing from it?
• Is past success always a fair label for future potential?
• Where could this AI make a mistake that affects a real student?

Your Task

  1. Answer in 3 to 5 sentences. Pick the dataset you would trust more for the first shortlist. Defend your choice using data quality, labels, variety, and representativeness.
  2. Reply to two classmates. Engage with their ideas in 2 to 3 sentences each.
  3. Be specific. Name a real student, school experience, skill, or situation the AI might miss.

What Good Looks Like

I would trust Student B's dataset more for the first shortlist because it includes more of the full applicant pool, not just people who already got the internship. Student B's trade-off is that the labels are harder to interpret because rejection does not always mean someone was unqualified. I would still want a human to review the AI's shortlist before any student loses an opportunity.

Why this works: Clear position, specific data reasoning, and a trade-off between clean labels and representative examples.
