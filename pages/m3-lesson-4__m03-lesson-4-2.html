Real-World Story

From medical AI to the Model Card

In 2018 the first 'Model Card' was published as a way to honestly describe what a model does, what it does not do, who is in the training data, and who is not. Within a year, major AI labs adopted the format. Your Model Card this week is the same format used in real industry. The discipline you build here is the discipline that prevents medical, hiring, and safety failures.

Core Idea

A finished model is one with honest limits, not a perfect one.

You will never close every gap. The goal is to fix the worst gap, retest, and then document the remaining weaknesses clearly enough that a stranger could trust your honesty.

A model that knows its limits is more useful than one that pretends it has none.

Key Takeaways

What you should remember

Add targeted training samples to fix the largest off-diagonal mistake from your confusion matrix.

Stress-test your model with edge cases: unusual angles, lighting, partial views.

Write a Model Card that names strengths, weaknesses, and one sentence about when you would and would not trust the model.
