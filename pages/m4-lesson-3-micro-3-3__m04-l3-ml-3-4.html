Bias in Outputs

What goes into training is what comes out, amplified.

Big Question

When an LLM produces a stereotyped answer, where is the bias coming from?
Watch → Compare → Apply

Before You Watch

Bias in output traces back to training data.

A model trained on the internet absorbs the internet's patterns, including its stereotypes. Even careful filtering does not catch everything, especially for groups underrepresented in the training data.

Watch

Micro-Learning Video 3.3

Concept Companion

Three ways training data becomes biased output.

All three of these happen quietly. The model is not 'racist' or 'sexist' in any conscious sense. It is reproducing the patterns it was trained on.

Some groups underrepresented

Representation gap

Output for these groups is lower-quality, vaguer, or stereotyped.

Patterns amplified

Stereotype reinforcement

Common stereotypes in training data become 'default' outputs.

Default worldview

Cultural framing

Models trained mostly in one language default to that culture's perspective.

Compare

Same prompt, different names.

A common test for output bias: change just the name in a prompt and compare what the model returns. The differences are the bias.

PromptCommon pattern in outputWhat this reveals
'Tell me about a doctor named [Anglo name]'Detailed, positive, specificDefault treated as 'normal'
'Tell me about a doctor named [non-Western name]'Often shorter, vaguer, or stereotypedUnderrepresentation in training data
'A nurse said...' (no name)Often 'she said...'Profession-gender stereotype amplification

Apply

Play: Same Prompt, Different Name

Ask an LLM the same question with three different names. Compare the answers. Note what changed and what stayed the same. Those differences are the bias.
