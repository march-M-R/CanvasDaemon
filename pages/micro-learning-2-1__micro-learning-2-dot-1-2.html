2.1 AI Before the Hype

Core Ideas

Three things to know about early AI

Early Focus

Symbolic AI

Early systems tried to represent intelligence with symbols, logic, and rules written by people.

Pattern: If X happens, then do Y.

Early Success

Expert Systems

Expert systems tried to capture specialist knowledge in software and return recommendations.

Example: Rule-based medical or technical advice.

Big Limitation

Brittleness

Many early systems worked only in narrow situations and failed when reality became messy.

Problem: Too many exceptions to write every rule by hand.

Early AI vs modern AI

Question Early rule-based AI Modern data-driven AI
How does it know what to do? Humans write rules directly. The system learns patterns from data.
What is it good at? Structured, predictable problems. Pattern-heavy tasks with many examples.
Main weakness Breaks when the situation does not match the rule. Can fail if the data is biased or incomplete.
Open: Why early AI struggled

Limited Data

There were not enough large digital datasets to train systems effectively.

Limited Compute

Computers were too weak to train or run large modern-style AI systems.

Messy Reality

Real-world problems contain ambiguity, change, and exceptions.
