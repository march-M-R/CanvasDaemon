How Did AI
Learn to Think?

In Lecture 1, you learned how to separate AI from regular software and automation. In this lecture, you will go deeper into the one idea that made modern AI possible: learning from feedback.

Lecture 2 of 4 Approx. 55–60 min Builds on L1

Big Question

What changed that made AI suddenly become powerful?

AI was discussed for decades before it became useful in everyday life. This lesson explains why early AI struggled, why machine learning changed the field, and why deep learning created the modern AI era.

Learning Objectives

1. Explain feedback loops

Describe how AI systems improve from examples, corrections, and previous results.

2. Compare ML and deep learning

Explain how machine learning and deep learning differ in the way they learn from data.

3. Identify why AI accelerated

Connect the rise of modern AI to data, computing power, GPUs, internet platforms, and new architectures.

Lesson Flow

Part 1: Opening Hook — Why did AI take so long?

You will begin with a short question: If scientists imagined AI in the 1950s, why did it only become powerful recently?

Part 2: The Feedback Loop

You will compare fixed automation with systems that improve from feedback, using spam filters as the central example.

Part 3: Four Moments That Changed AI

You will trace the shift from early thinking machines to expert systems, machine learning, and deep learning.

Part 4: Machine Learning vs Deep Learning

You will learn the difference between models that rely on human-designed features and deep learning systems that discover features from large amounts of data.
